Original slice tuning choices (authorable, not final balance)
Current authored trials use 500 health, launch speed 1000 m/s, 65-degree cone half-angles, and
muzzles at X = +/-3 meters on the authored voxel model. The converted 200 m/s ballistic references below remain
solver fixtures. See src/docs/combat.md for current tuning and measurements.
- Kestrel maximum health: 50; Rusty cannon reload: 2 seconds per mounted weapon.
- Rusty cannon launch speed: 200 meters/second. Apply constant downward
  acceleration of 30 meters/second squared, authored on its weapon definition.
  This is stylized projectile gravity, independent of Godot's global gravity.
  Speed changes along the arc: vertical motion slows on ascent and speeds up on
  descent. No drag, homing, inherited shooter velocity, or damage scaling by speed.
- Prefer the earliest ballistic intercept, producing a direct, low cannon arc.
  At equal muzzle/target height, a stationary target 600 meters away needs about
  13.4 degrees of elevation, 3.08 seconds of flight, and a 35.7-meter rise. At
  1000 meters, these become 24.3 degrees, 5.49 seconds, and a 112.9-meter rise.
  These are authoring references; inspect the resulting weight and curve in play.
- The former 9.81-unit/s² comparison converts to 98.1 m/s² and permits about
  408 meters of level range. Gravity 30 allows 1333 meters theoretically, leaving margin
  for the authored 1000-meter targeting range. Elevated/moving targets can still
  be unreachable; being in range alone does not guarantee a firing solution.
- Range is muzzle-to-target distance, not distance traveled along the curve.
  Give cannonballs an independent 8-second maximum lifetime; a five-second expiry
  would remove a valid 1000-meter shot before impact. Misses continue falling until
  impact or expiry, and can intercept ships beyond the targeting range.
- Begin testing engagement at 600 meters from the target, leaving room inside range.
- Kestrel broadside cones use an 89-degree half-angle (178-degree full opening),
  widened from the initial 85-degree trial after primitive-model inspection.

10. PROJECTILES, HIT DETECTION, AND PERFORMANCE DESIGN
Chosen starting approach to implement in item 11:
- Treat the Rusty cannonball as a point mass under constant downward acceleration.
  Its visible path and collision path share the same ballistic positions; do not
  animate a decorative curve over a straight-moving damage projectile.
- For aiming, let r = target_position - muzzle_position, u = target world velocity,
  a = Vector3(0, -gravity, 0), and s = launch speed. Find the earliest positive
  time t within the lifetime satisfying:
      length_squared(r + u*t - 0.5*a*t*t) = s*s*t*t
  Then v0 = (r + u*t - 0.5*a*t*t) / t. Use a bounded one-dimensional root search
  with a checked intercept residual; handle near-tangent roots at the edge of
  reachability, not just sign changes. Reject non-finite/invalid solutions.
  Apply range/cone eligibility to that earliest intercept; do not select a high
  lob to bypass a rejected low arc. No artillery-mode or general ballistics
  framework is needed. The solver assumes constant target velocity only for aim;
  the projectile is independent of the target after launch.
- A journey-owned projectile controller advances a typed collection once per
  physics tick. Each record holds position, velocity, gravity, remaining lifetime,
  damage, and source faction/identity captured at launch; shots survive shooter
  death. For dt clipped to the remaining lifetime, advance exactly under constant
  acceleration: next_position = position + velocity*dt + 0.5*a*dt*dt, then
  next_velocity = velocity + a*dt. Never renormalize velocity to the launch speed.
- Draw small shared-mesh cannonballs. No RigidBody3D, Area3D, per-shot Timer, or
  individual physics callback is needed. Keep physics queries on the main thread.
- Sweep each short arc segment from its previous to next ballistic position using
  Godot's direct physics-space ray query, including the final lifetime-clipped
  step. Process segments in flight order and stop at the first hit against existing
  ship capsules/island colliders. Never raycast just from muzzle to predicted hit.
  The maximum arc-to-chord error is gravity*dt*dt/8; at gravity 30 and the accepted 30 Hz it is
  about 0.0042 meters. One query per normal physics tick is sufficient; subdivide
  longer steps if needed to keep this error below 0.1 meters. No projectile-by-ship
  scan or custom collision engine is needed.
- Include ships of both factions in the query mask and exclude the firing ship.
  A friendly hit consumes the projectile but deals zero damage. A hostile hit
  consumes it and applies 5 damage once. Island hits consume it without damage.
  "Explode" means impact and removal in this effects-free slice; no splash damage.
- Expire misses after the authored 8-second lifetime. Do not expire a valid arc
  because its path exceeds 1000 meters or its flight exceeds range / launch speed.
  Terrain and water currently have no colliders; do not introduce either as part
  of combat. Projectiles do not collide with other projectiles.
- Segment checks cover projectile travel between ticks, not full continuous
  collision between two moving shapes. Verify crossing targets at the supported
  speeds/tick rate; do not claim arbitrary-speed continuous collision support.
- Keep target acquisition and opportunistic searches on a staggered, bounded
  schedule (start at 0.2 seconds); steering, firing validation, and projectile
  movement run at physics frequency. Apply cheap faction/range filters before
  solving trajectories. Bound candidate solves per ready slot/search, rotating
  through candidates without starvation; retry failed solutions on the scheduled
  search rather than solving every ship/weapon pair every tick. Revalidate a
  selected shot at firing time. Reuse registered ships and shared resources.
- Store projectile positions relative to a single registered projectile root.
  Rebase that root once and derive global query endpoints after rebasing; never
  retain stale global previous positions that produce a world-spanning sweep.
  Keep velocity, downward acceleration, and remaining lifetime unchanged by a
  rebase; reset interpolation with existing origin contracts. Combat goals use
  target references/local offsets or recomputation rather than stale coordinates.
- Use straightforward GDScript and ordinary shared MeshInstance3D visuals.
  Profiling justified conservative spatial neighbor queries for ship avoidance
  and loaded islands. Pooling and MultiMesh remain deferred. Preserve existing
  movement/terrain optimizations and compare filtering against full-scan results.
- At 220 ships, two cannons each, a 2-second reload, and 8-second lifetime, firing
  continuously with no impacts gives about 1,760 active shots / 220 shots per
  second. Include synchronized volleys and unreachable moving targets to measure
  both projectile work and failed aiming solves. These estimates do not establish
  performance. Measure filtered ship-pair avoidance alongside combat.
