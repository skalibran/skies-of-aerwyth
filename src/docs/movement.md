# Fleet movement

## Persistent marker

`FleetController` owns one authored `FleetMarker` in [Journey](../../scenes/world/journey.tscn). The purple four-meter cube marks the center of the fleet's navigation sphere. It starts at `(0, 3800, 100)`, with the initial ships retaining their offsets around it. The first wave milestone is 100 meters ahead at logical Z = 0. Later milestones are at Z = -1000, -2000, and so on. Journey progress starts at zero and measures travel from the initial marker position. Its X and Y never follow ship positions. Ordinary travel changes only Z, toward Z-. There is no averaged fleet-center node, jumping destination anchor, or regrouping state.

Journey progression, camera tracking and viewing bounds, scenery streaming, water coverage, and origin rebasing all reference this same marker. Membership changes cannot teleport it or advance progression. A next encounter is a route milestone, not another movement owner.

`EncounterDirector.is_combat_active()` supplies fleet-wide combat state: enabled pending spawn plans or any living hostile hold combat active. Disabling Journey combat bypasses that state. The marker continues moving during combat, blocked placement, and partly spawned waves. Combat state still prevents sphere contraction and refreshes navigation when the encounter clears. It does not change travel speed. An empty friendly fleet stops.

Forward steps cross wave milestones without capping speed. The director consumes each crossed milestone on the next physics tick. Waves can overlap while previous enemies remain alive. See [encounters](encounters.md) for scheduling and guarantees.

## Sphere size and travel pace

The starter minimum radius is 180 meters. `required_radius()` takes the larger of that minimum, maneuvering room for the least agile hull, and a footprint-based size: 1.5 times the square root of the sum of `(enclosing hull radius + spacing)^2` for every living ship. Default spacing is 35 meters. Large ships need more room than small ships. Both friendly and enemy hulls contribute through `FleetController.occupants`. The separate `members` registry contains only friendlies and governs travel pace and whether the player fleet is empty.

Radius changes at 30 meters/second. Growth is permitted during combat. Contraction waits until combat has ended and every surviving hull fits within the requested smaller sphere, including its safety margin. Goals reserve the ship's enclosing hull radius plus eight meters inside the fleet boundary.

Cruise speed is 25 m/s with 3 m/s^2 acceleration. It is limited by the slowest member's maximum speed minus the 12 m/s local maneuver allowance. A ship's distance from the sphere does not reduce travel speed: trailing and newly spawned ships use arrival assistance to catch up while the marker continues moving. Friendly navigators inside the sphere report a safe marker speed for their obstacle routes. The smallest allowance limits travel. Approaching ships outside the sphere route around their own obstacles without holding the distant marker. An enemy route cannot slow the marker. The marker has no simulated inertia and can stop immediately for blocked local routes or an empty friendly fleet without changing ship momentum.

## Arrival boost

Living ships outside the fleet sphere receive a distance-based approach boost, including both friendly and enemy cloud spawns. `FleetController` owns **Arrival Boost Distance** (1000 meters) and **Arrival Max Multiplier** (10). The multiplier rises linearly from 1 at the sphere surface to 10 at 1000 meters outside, then stays capped. Distance includes altitude and uses the current sphere radius. It is unchanged by origin rebasing. Setting the maximum multiplier to 1 disables assistance.

The same multiplier scales navigation's local approach speed and flight's maximum speed, climb speed, acceleration, and braking. It returns to 1 at the boundary without changing authored ship stats or retaining a spawn state. Yaw and lateral drag keep their normal tuning. Fleet travel still uses the ordinary friendly speed limits and obstacle-route allowances. Island queries include the faster requested motion, while lookahead retains base braking for conservative planning as the boost tapers. Physics still owns momentum and contacts.

## Navigation

Each Airship owns one [ShipNavigation](../../scripts/ships/ship_navigation.gd). Its final destination is an offset from the marker, inside the sphere with hull clearance. The same navigator handles travel, combat, island clearance, separation corrections, and containment. ShipTravel and the separate island-detour controller have been removed.

Ships outside the usable sphere first attempt a direct approach. When an isle blocks it, the navigator samples six approach waypoints around, above, or below the closest obstruction. Both the waypoint segment and the requested course must clear loaded islands. The next segment must also pass that obstruction. A waypoint is stored relative to its isle, so rebasing preserves it and unloading invalidates it. The navigator retains a clear detour, abandons it as soon as direct approach becomes possible, and spaces failed detour searches by 0.5 seconds. Separation remains active, but cannot reverse direct inward progress. These temporary approach legs may lie outside the sphere. Final destinations remain inside it. Approaching ships do not run the local 32-candidate search at five marker speeds each tick or constrain marker pace. Their leg lookahead ends at the waypoint rather than extrapolating through scenery beyond the planned turn. This is bounded local routing, not a complete solver for arbitrary mazes.

For ordinary movement, a ship samples up to 32 nearby candidate destinations, normally one 100-meter step from its local sampling center, including explicit lateral, vertical, forward and inward choices plus its retained goal. Candidates must fit inside the sphere with hull clearance. Both the short waypoint segment and its combined world-motion lookahead must clear nearby island capsules before scoring. Lookahead reserves braking/turn time and the time local movement needs to clear an island's inflated radius, so a broad detour begins before slowing is necessary. Lift is limited to the actual climb speed. Scoring favors manageable turns and inward movement when predicted momentum approaches the edge. On arrival, a fresh destination produces natural movement within the fleet. Changing combat state or selected target also refreshes the destination. While moving, combat destinations are reconsidered every three seconds, or sooner on arrival or a target change. Displaced ships and enemies entering from outside sample inside the sphere and approach it through this same controller.

The turn-preference score compares the hull's propulsion axis with the requested horizontal world velocity, including marker travel. A request with no horizontal movement preserves the current heading. Local destinations ahead of the hull receive no separate forward-progress reward: the marker already supplies that travel. The sphere bounds destinations but does not allocate formation positions or reward unoccupied space. Separation remains a close-range correction.

Combat selects from the ship's authored target-relative positions in fixed fleet axes. Front is Z- and up is Y+, independently of hull yaw. The enemy bearing is inverted to obtain our offset, so Manta's `bottom` places it above the enemy. Engagement Distance defaults to 150 meters with 20% tolerance and small angular spread around each enabled direction. Gun ranges and cones do not influence this selection. Exact firing permission remains with mounted weapons. See [combat positioning](combat.md#target-selection-and-navigation).

The navigator retains a valid attack position and follows the target's translation. Its endpoint must fit inside the hull-cleared sphere and clear island and target hulls. Successive local waypoints approach distant endpoints. Transit may cross other sectors. Direct steps are tried first, then up to 32 local alternatives that reduce endpoint distance. Retained points are revalidated against moving targets and scenery. A failed position tries its distance alternatives and other enabled positions, including the opposite broadside. When all fail, the navigator resumes ordinary random goal selection while retaining the enemy and retrying at the three-second combat interval. Weapons continue independent firing throughout this fallback. Separation, island routing, and final containment remain with the same navigator.

The current target selector retains the nearest eligible opponent it acquired. A future priority selector can supply an opponent anywhere in the sphere. Local step distance never filters targets. Opponents within the sphere plus the 600-meter entry band can influence combat, but their attack positions must still fit inside the sphere. The island query includes the possible attack endpoints as well as the local course and world-motion lookahead. This remains bounded local routing and can fall back to wandering when no improving attack route is found.

Desired velocity combines marker velocity with local movement. Ship separation is considered before final containment and island clearance. Containment predicts using current relative speed, braking, and yaw response, then limits the requested relative endpoint to the sphere. This anticipates turns and reduces outward commands before reaching its edge. The marker translates during combat, carrying the sphere and every local destination forward.

**The guarantee concerns intentional navigation.** Final destinations and local obstacle alternatives are inside the sphere. Momentum and contacts may carry a physical hull outside. Its navigator then approaches through clear legs around external scenery. No body positions or velocities are clamped to the boundary. Inside the sphere, planning first tries the intended marker pace, independently of its current stopped or reduced speed. If needed, it retries at 75%, 50%, 25%, then zero of that pace. A viable slower route permits continued local movement. If no candidate route is available, the ship brakes. A friendly local route with zero travel allowance holds the marker, and a later clear route releases that constraint through normal marker acceleration. The local sampler is not a global route solver. Geometry that blocks every route within the sphere can halt travel.

Island candidate filtering covers the full sampled goal segment and the maximum steering lookahead. Its braking bound includes actual world and relative momentum plus intended travel, including contact speeds above the propulsion limit. Displaced hulls also reserve the distance back to their local sampling center. This preserves the spatial cache while keeping relevant islands available for exact path checks. The exact island check intersects horizontal clearance with the segment's overlapping height interval, allowing a climb or descent that clears the island before reaching it. Terrain is outside navigation by design: authored terrain heights and supported fleet sizes keep it below the navigation volume. Large scale fixtures deliberately exceed normal content bounds. The runtime does not enforce terrain separation.

## Forward flight and momentum

Airships remain upright `RigidBody3D` hulls. Godot/Jolt owns position, linear/angular velocity, and contacts. [ShipFlight](../../scripts/ships/ship_flight.gd) submits mass-scaled propulsion, lift and lateral drag, plus inertia-scaled yaw torque. Turning builds and brakes gradually. Poor alignment reduces forward thrust. Altitude has separate lift control. The navigator uses the same yaw and braking tuning to anticipate boundary maneuvers.

Kestrel's authored limits are 50 m/s speed, 5 m/s climb, 4/6 m/s^2 acceleration/braking, 12?/s yaw, 10?/s? yaw acceleration, and 0.7/s lateral drag. Contacts can exceed commanded limits temporarily and transfer momentum without damage. Death removes all flight control and restores passive wreck physics, as described in [combat](combat.md). Scene placement and floating-origin translation are the only routine transform assignments.

## Ownership and tick order

1. Journey removes queued deaths, processes friendly spawn requests, and steps authored encounters.
2. FleetController prepares sphere size, combat state, and intended travel speed from friendly propulsion. It does not move the marker yet.
3. Airship evaluates its selected opponent and prepares navigation. Shared separation reads one completed physics snapshot for all ships.
4. Each navigator plans its bounded obstacle route against intended travel and reports a safe speed. FleetController takes the friendly minimum and advances the marker once.
5. Projectiles resolve lethal impacts. Each living navigator revalidates its goal for the actual marker motion, applies separation, and checks the final contained course. Invalid separation is discarded. Another bounded goal is tried when the base route is blocked. ShipFlight submits control once. Mounted weapons validate and fire independently.
6. Journey removes casualties, shifts registered roots when needed, and refreshes progression and scenery. Native physics integrates the submitted forces.

IslandSpawner retains its cached spatial query. Outside-sphere arrivals cache a waypoint relative to their blocking island and revalidate the route each tick. Unloading that island invalidates the waypoint. Relative goals and waypoints survive a 10240-meter origin shift unchanged, as do physical velocities and RNG state.

## Camera and debug

Fleet camera focus and its 9000-meter viewing sphere use FleetMarker. Free flight inherits marker translation while preserving its offset and independent view. Following an individual ship also remains inside that viewing sphere. The initial orbit is 600 meters. Wheel zoom changes by 25 meters and controller zoom uses 300 m/s.

| Input | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Left click a living ship | Follow that ship. |
| WASD / left stick | Free flight. Forward follows view elevation. |
| RMB + mouse / right stick | Orbit a tracked target or look in free mode. |
| Wheel / D-pad up/down | Zoom while tracking. |
| Shift / LT | Triple movement and zoom speed. |
| F3 | Toggle the navigation sphere, ship goals, velocity arrows, and selected ship's target line. |

UI focus reserves camera input. Cancel or world pointer input releases it. Losing application focus releases mouse capture. `focus_fleet()` and the `camera_focus_fleet` action exist without a default keyboard/controller binding. Mouse selection is the current ship-selection input.

The marker cube stays visible independently of F3. Navigation debug starts hidden. F3 toggles the purple sphere together with travel goals in cyan, combat goals in red, the final requested velocity in orange, and actual velocity in green. A magenta line connects the selected ship to its current main combat target, using interpolated ship positions. It disappears when selection clears or either ship dies or is removed. [FleetNavigationSphere](../../scripts/fleet/fleet_navigation_sphere.gd) draws a sparse wireframe of latitude and meridian rings, centered on the marker and scaled to FleetController's current radius. Its unit mesh is built once and inherits marker travel, physics interpolation, and origin shifts. These visuals only read navigation state and consume no randomness.

## Validation

Use the absolute paths, isolated process APPDATA, and external logs required by [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). Focused scripts run with `--headless --fixed-fps 30 --script res://scripts/tools/<check>.gd`. Physics remains at the project's 30 Hz.

- `check_movement`: fixed marker axes, continuous combat travel, uninterrupted milestone crossing, growth from both factions, friendly-only pace, safe contraction, continuous travel with trailing or distant ships, empty-fleet stopping, camera input, UI focus, debug, and rebasing.
- `check_ship_flight`: physical thrust/turning/braking, contacts, impulse recovery, passive wrecks, authored attack spacing and directions, alternate positions, wandering and attack retries, weapon-independent movement, equipment changes, moving Manta climbs with pass-by fire and mirrored upper-position objectives, bounded combat and separation, inward recovery, and approaching a selected far-side target despite a nearer opponent. A 48-Kestrel travel fixture checks that native movement retains spread on all three axes instead of collapsing toward the front of the sphere. Arrival checks cover distance and altitude, the cap, rebasing, unchanged authored tuning, and faster native-physics approaches for both factions that taper to normal speed at the boundary.
- `check_island_navigation`: bounded obstacle choices, world-motion detours, safe slowing at intended pace, same-tick blockage/recovery, clear over/underflight, rebase stability, and unloading.
- `check_ship_arrival`: a queued burst of 12 friendlies alongside 12 enemies concealed by isles, native approaches into the moving sphere, contained final goals, bounded instantiation work, and cached detour rebasing/unloading. It reports script timings. Append `-- --visual` with an external `AERWYTH_CAPTURE_DIR` for rendered captures.
- `check_encounters`: normal travel through two milestones, overlapping waves, bounded goals during moving combat, pending-spawn combat state, plus encounter content and scheduling.

The spread regression starts 48 Kestrels on an 80-meter, 4-by-3-by-4 lattice centered on the marker, at 25 m/s cruise, with normal navigation and separation but no combat or scenery. The fleet radius is approximately 525 meters. In a 60-second comparison at 30 Hz, the previous local-course reward pulled the mean position 325 meters forward and compressed Z standard deviation to 21 meters. Scoring the requested world heading reduced that offset to approximately 55 meters and retained 116 meters of Z standard deviation. The check asserts bounded centroid drift and spread on every axis, independently of exact trajectories. A separate 120-second, 24-versus-24 Kestrel comparison retained firing opportunities for every ship and increased combined Z standard deviation from 110 to 173 meters. Rendered side/top views still showed target-relative battle lines. These fixtures establish removal of the shared forward pileup, not uniform volume occupation or full moving-combat acceptance. See [navigation follow-ups](todo/navigation-encounters.txt).

The arrival fixture starts Kestrels 2000 meters from the marker, including 1200 meters of altitude difference. With continuous marker movement and the default boost, friendly and enemy ships reached the sphere in approximately 46 and 48 seconds. Unassisted references remained approximately 1065 and 969 meters outside after 75 seconds. The fixture verifies forward marker movement on every tick and sustained cruise after initial acceleration. These are focused arrival results, not combat-scale performance measurements.

For rendered inspection, run `check_encounters` without `--headless`, set an external `AERWYTH_CAPTURE_DIR`, and append `-- --visual`. `marker-battle.png` shows the marker, navigation sphere and combat destinations. Scale/performance and long travel are separate opt-in checks. Earlier measurements do not validate this navigation implementation.
