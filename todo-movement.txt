SKIES OF AERWYTH - INITIAL MOVEMENT IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Status: Planning only. Every implementation item below is outstanding.
Design authority: src/game_design_document.md.
Current authorization: edit the GDD and this file only. Scene, script, resource,
project-setting, and AGENTS.md changes below describe future implementation.

SCOPE AND DECISIONS

- Build a primitive ship, orbit camera, primitive island, fleet movement, a
  floating origin, and occasional islands along forward travel (Z-).
- The persistent fleet anchor drives movement and owns journey progression.
  The average of active friendly ship positions regulates its forward speed.
- Fleet camera tracking remains an evaluation choice: average ship position
  for visual centering, or the anchor for a steadier, slightly forward focus.
  Support both as an editor setting for comparison; neither changes progression.
- Ordinary camera movement stays inside a sphere around the fleet and never
  pauses the simulation.
- Island management, island panning, cloud transitions, and their gameplay
  pause are documented in the GDD only and are outside this implementation plan.
- Combat, weapons, production, building placement, atmosphere animation, and
  a complete save/load system are also outside this first movement milestone.
- Controller ship selection remains TBD. Do not invent a selection scheme.
- Values such as speeds, radii, and angles below are tunable prototype choices,
  not final balance. Ask before resolving remaining material gameplay decisions.

1. SCENE COMPOSITION AND OWNERSHIP

[ ] Compose scenes/world/journey.tscn as the eventual playable prototype, with
    matching orchestration under scripts/world/. Add lighting and a simple
    environment sufficient to inspect movement; no art generation is needed.
[ ] Compose scenes/ships/ship.tscn, scenes/islands/island.tscn, and
    scenes/camera/orbit_camera.tscn with scripts in matching feature folders.
    Add a fleet scene under scenes/fleet/ if needed for its anchor and references.
    Create only files with a concrete responsibility; no empty feature framework.
[ ] Keep the journey owner responsible for world lifetime, origin shifts, island
    loading, and registration of all live ships for avoidance. Keep friendly
    fleet membership, the anchor, and the calculated mean with a FleetController.
    Each ship owns its velocity and navigation offset. The camera owns
    selection/follow state and free-pan state.
[ ] Keep ship bodies and islands independent of the translating anchor in the
    scene tree. Moving the anchor must not translate ships automatically.
    Keep the world container transform small and stable as travel continues.
[ ] Use exported typed references and explicit registration/unregistration for
    members, targets, and shiftable roots. Do not scan the scene tree every tick
    or infer another object's methods from strings. No autoload is needed yet.
[ ] Author tunable movement/camera values as exported properties; use a typed
    Resource if several instances share a coherent movement definition. Shared
    definitions stay immutable; velocity, goals, and RNG state are per instance.
[ ] Once the scene exists, register the startup scene and required Input Map
    actions in project.godot. Update AGENTS.md's baseline only when implementation
    actually introduces those systems, not during this documentation task.

2. GENERIC SHIP SCENE (a)

[ ] Use one base ship scene for all factions and eventual hull/weapon choices.
    Fleet membership is supplied externally; movement must not require a weapon
    or a specific hull. Do not implement combat or catalogs to fill placeholders.
[ ] Use a CharacterBody3D root in MOTION_MODE_FLOATING with explicit scripted
    acceleration and velocity, no gravity or floor snapping. This gives control
    over heavy motion without relying on rigid-body impulses or angular physics.
[ ] Add a VisualRoot and exactly two visible primitives: an elongated capsule
    mesh aligned with Z for the envelope, and a smaller box below as the gondola.
    Orient the ship's forward direction along local -Z.
[ ] Start with a compact capsule collision shape aligned to the hull, allowing
    close side-by-side passes without an oversized bounding sphere. Use spherical
    distance checks for the broader avoidance neighborhood described in section 6.
    Size the collider to cover the intended solid silhouette and inspect it in
    motion. This is the prototype alternative to a single spherical collider.
[ ] Keep physical translation owned by the body. Use VisualRoot for controlled
    pitch and bank; its presentation must never write back into navigation.
    Keep the model centered on its collider rather than leaving it behind.
[ ] Expose a stable entity ID and a typed selection/follow target. Reuse the
    physical collider for mouse picking where adequate; use an explicit ship
    collision layer/mask so scenery and UI cannot be mistaken for ships.

3. PERSISTENT ANCHOR AND AVERAGE POSITION (d)

[ ] Maintain a registered collection of active friendly ships. Calculate their
    arithmetic mean once per physics step; enemy ships must not affect it.
    Initialize the anchor from the initial formation once. Later membership
    changes update the mean without reinitializing or teleporting the anchor.
[ ] Give the anchor a constant forward direction Vector3(0, 0, -1), a tunable
    cruise speed, and an acceleration limit. Its X/Y position stays on the route
    for this prototype. Ships may move locally around it in all three dimensions.
[ ] Use distance to the mean as speed feedback. Starting mathematical pseudocode:
      gap = length(anchor_position - average_ship_position)
      excess = max(0, gap - comfortable_gap)
      ratio = excess / slowdown_distance
      target_speed = cruise_speed / (1 + ratio * ratio)
    Require slowdown_distance > 0. Approach target_speed with bounded
    acceleration, then advance the anchor along -Z. The function remains
    nonnegative, slows smoothly, and allows ships to catch up. Tune the curve
    against a lagging fleet; do not create a hard tether or snap ships forward.
[ ] Give ships enough maximum speed to catch up with the anchor while making
    small local maneuvers. Avoid an equilibrium in which all ships permanently
    trail an unreachable destination.
[ ] With no active friendly ships, stop advancing the anchor and retain a valid
    camera fallback position. Do not divide by zero or decide defeat/wipe rules.
[ ] Define one fixed-step order: snapshot ship positions and velocities; derive
    the mean and advance the anchor; calculate ship steering from that snapshot;
    move each ship once; refresh presentation data; perform any origin shift at
    the agreed boundary. The controller orchestrates named operations; ships
    must not also run a second independent movement update.

4. LOCAL NAVIGATION DESTINATIONS (d.1, d.2)

[ ] Store each travel destination as an offset from the anchor. Its scene-space
    destination is anchor_position + navigation_offset. An origin shift does
    not change this offset, and an advancing anchor carries the destination.
[ ] Sample initial offsets in a tunable formation volume, avoiding initial
    collider overlap. No NavigationRegion3D or baked ground navmesh is needed
    for the initial open-air movement prototype.
[ ] On reaching an arrival radius, choose another offset near the ship's current
    anchor-relative position. Limit the per-choice displacement and keep normal
    destinations inside the formation volume. Do not resample across the entire
    formation when a ship reaches a point at its front edge.
[ ] Use a minimum goal separation greater than the arrival tolerance and a short
    retarget interval to avoid churning random goals every tick. Bound candidate
    retries. If a ship lies outside the formation, steer back gradually using a
    reachable recovery destination rather than teleporting or retrying forever.
[ ] Seek destinations relative to the moving anchor: include anchor velocity
    when constructing the ship's preferred velocity, then apply speed and
    acceleration limits. Arrival must be measured in that moving reference frame
    so ships can reach their destinations even while the fleet advances.
[ ] Keep the destination provider distinct from steering. The GDD's future
    combat pursuit can supply a destination through the same movement contract;
    travel wandering must not compete with it. Do not implement combat yet.

5. SLUGGISH AIRSHIP MOTION (d.4)

[ ] Apply bounded acceleration, braking, climb speed, and yaw rate. Allow gentle
    lateral drift rather than forcing every small waypoint change into a tight
    forward-facing turn. Keep all rates independent of render frame rate.
[ ] Use the actual velocity after collision handling as movement feedback.
    Call move_and_slide() once per physics step with velocity in units/second;
    do not multiply that velocity by delta before passing it to the body.
[ ] Keep the body upright with controlled yaw. Turn toward horizontal travel
    gradually, preserving heading when horizontal speed is near zero. Never
    apply unrestricted look_at() toward a vertically displaced destination.
[ ] Animate VisualRoot pitch from climb/descent and bank from turning, with
    small exported angle limits and gradual settling. Return toward level flight
    when the maneuver ends. Pitch and bank cannot accumulate into loops or flips.
[ ] Keep visual position tied to the physical root so the hull cannot drift
    outside its collision envelope. A separately animated visual child is enough;
    do not introduce a second authoritative ship position.

6. AVOIDANCE AND CLOSE PASS-BYS (d.3)

[ ] Combine destination seeking with local predictive separation. Use a spherical
    neighborhood around each ship to find nearby ships from the registered
    position/velocity snapshot; inspect every relevant nearby faction.
[ ] Predict closest approach over a short time horizon using relative position
    and relative velocity, accounting for hull clearance. Steer away before
    contact and increase separation strongly near collision distance. Handle
    coincident positions and near-zero relative velocity without division errors.
[ ] Keep the soft comfort distance only moderately larger than physical hull
    clearance. Relax avoidance for ships whose predicted paths are safely
    separating, allowing close parallel and opposing passes.
[ ] Bound the avoidance contribution and preserve acceleration limits. For a
    symmetric head-on encounter, use a consistent passing convention and stable
    IDs to break exact overlaps/ties; do not choose a new random side every tick.
[ ] Let solid-body collision remain the final overlap safeguard. Inspect capsule
    rotation and visual banking under contact so the slender hulls do not visibly
    clip even when the movement centerlines remain separated.
[ ] Start with a simple neighbor pass for a small prototype fleet. Measure its
    cost before adding a spatial grid; avoid per-frame scene scans and temporary
    node creation. Add a broad phase only when demonstrated fleet size needs it.

7. ORBIT CAMERA, SELECTION, AND VIEWING SPHERE (b.1-b.3)

[ ] Give one CameraController explicit FLEET, SHIP, and FREE modes. Its authored
    rig contains an orbit focus, yaw/pitch controls, and Camera3D at an orbit
    distance. Clamp pitch to avoid inversion. Preserve orbit orientation when
    changing targets, while snapping the focus onto the newly selected target.
[ ] Expose the fleet focus source as an editor enum: AVERAGE or ANCHOR. Compare
    both in the prototype, including uneven formations and membership changes.
    Keep the choice marked unresolved until the user evaluates it.
[ ] On an unhandled left mouse click, raycast from Camera3D through the cursor
    using the ship picking mask. Resolve a typed ship reference and enter SHIP
    mode. UI-consumed clicks do not pick ships. An empty click does not silently
    invent a deselection or fleet-focus rule.
[ ] Add an on-screen Focus fleet action to restore FLEET mode. Its keyboard and
    controller bindings remain TBD; controller ship cycling/selection is absent.
    On removal of a tracked ship, return to FLEET mode safely. Disconnect target
    subscriptions and clear references when targets or the camera exit the scene.
[ ] Add Input Map actions with their consumers together:
      camera_pan_forward/back/left/right: W/S/A/D and left thumbstick axes.
      camera_rotate_hold: right mouse button, held rather than toggled.
      camera_look_left/right/up/down: right thumbstick axes.
      select_ship: left mouse button.
      camera_focus_fleet: shared action invoked by the fleet-focus UI.
[ ] Meaningful WASD/left-stick input enters FREE mode from the current focus,
    without a position jump. Map pan input to camera-yaw-relative horizontal
    movement; normalize diagonal input and apply a controller deadzone. Right
    stick rotation alone does not release tracking. Mouse motion rotates only
    while the right button is held; mouse motion and stick rates use appropriate
    units so the result does not depend on render frame rate.
[ ] Clear held rotation state on focus loss and restore normal cursor behavior
    on release. Do not let movement keys pan through a UI text field or let
    controller drift continuously release tracking.
[ ] Center the fleet viewing sphere on the calculated ship mean, independently
    of which point is chosen for FLEET tracking. Retain the last valid center
    when the fleet is empty. Expose a radius large enough for the prototype's
    formation and orbit clearance.
[ ] Constrain the final camera position, accounting for both free-pan offset and
    orbit distance, to this moving sphere. Enforce the boundary during panning,
    orbiting, following, and origin shifts. At the edge, clamp the requested
    displacement/available orbit distance without flipping the camera. Let the
    advancing sphere carry a released camera when it would otherwise fall out.
    Validate that selected ships throughout the formation can still be framed.
[ ] Camera movement, target selection, and sphere clamping must never pause the
    world, change anchor progression, or cause island spawning by themselves.
    Do not add a distance-triggered pause.

8. GENERIC ISLAND AND ROUTE SPAWNING (c, e)

[ ] Make island.tscn a reusable Node3D independent of its future resources and
    building spots. Use a flattened cylinder for its top and a downward-tapering
    cone/cylinder below it to read as a floating island. Use simple materials.
[ ] Give each island a stable ID, definition/shape parameters, and logical route
    position. Its visual instance is disposable. Building sockets, production,
    liberation rewards, and editing UI are not implemented by this prototype.
[ ] Spawn islands ahead along Z- using an exported positive spacing range and
    a bounded look-ahead distance. Use a dedicated RNG and a persistent next
    spawn coordinate. Neither camera movement nor origin shifts advance that RNG
    or reschedule content. Determine thresholds from the anchor's logical Z.
[ ] Put initial island scenery outside the ship travel corridor with enough
    clearance for its primitive geometry. Island obstacle navigation and ramming
    rules remain future design work; do not silently introduce those mechanics.
[ ] Load scenery that could enter the bounded fleet camera view, with a margin.
    Unload island visuals once they are sufficiently far behind that area.
    Retain island records so a later management view can reconstruct them.
    Long travel should keep live nodes bounded; persistent records may grow.
[ ] Prevent duplicate IDs/spawns at segment boundaries and handle crossing more
    than one spawn threshold in a step. Keep generation separate from scene
    instantiation so logical content does not depend on whether it is visible.
    Keep the spawn implementation specific to islands until another type exists.

9. LOGICAL COORDINATES AND FLOATING ORIGIN (e)

[ ] Represent logical Z as a signed 64-bit segment index plus a scalar offset
    normalized to [0, segment_length). Use floor-based normalization for negative
    travel: crossing below zero decrements the segment and wraps the offset.
    A starting segment length of 1024 units is a tuning choice, not game balance.
[ ] Store the active origin as origin_segment_z. For a nearby logical position:
      scene_z = (entity_segment_z - origin_segment_z) * segment_length + offset_z
    Subtract integer segments before conversion to a scene float. Do not first
    form an enormous absolute float or Vector3. Compare distant logical positions
    as segment/offset pairs; route progress increases as anchor logical Z decreases.
[ ] Keep one authoritative runtime position per object. For loaded moving ships,
    derive logical coordinates from body position and origin when needed; do not
    maintain an independently integrated second position. Island records own
    their fixed logical positions and generate scene transforms from those.
[ ] Rebase when the anchor passes a tunable local-Z threshold. Choose a shift S
    that is a whole number of segment lengths. At one physics boundary:
      origin_segment_z += S.z / segment_length
      each registered scene root's position -= S
    Choose the threshold/segment size to leave room for the fleet, camera sphere,
    and loaded scenery within the desired local precision budget.
[ ] Shift all live world roots exactly once: ships, islands, anchor, and camera
    rig. Shift cached scene-space means, free-camera focus, and smoothing history
    in the same operation. Descendants inherit their root's shift and must not
    be shifted again. Anchor-relative navigation offsets stay unchanged.
[ ] Preserve velocities, orientations, IDs, RNG state, timers, logical positions,
    selection, and spawn thresholds. Emit one typed origin-shift notification
    for owners of cached coordinates. Do not treat rebasing as traveled distance
    or derive velocity from a discontinuity in raw scene positions.
[ ] After moving affected roots, reset physics interpolation when enabled, and
    update any custom camera interpolation history. Verify with the configured
    Jolt backend; a visually hidden jump alone does not prove physics correctness.
[ ] Bound X/Y movement in this prototype through formation and camera limits.
    Rebasing only Z does not solve an infinitely wide active region. Do not
    introduce a general multidimensional world-streaming framework in advance.

PERSISTENCE BOUNDARY FOR LATER SAVE WORK

The movement milestone does not implement save files. Preserve these contracts:
- Future snapshots need the anchor's logical position and speed; each ship's
  stable ID, definition, logical position, rotation, velocity, navigation offset,
  and relevant RNG state; island records; and the spawner's next coordinate/RNG.
- Save camera mode and selected stable ID if restoring the view. Do not persist
  scene paths, instance IDs, or giant world-space transforms as entity identity.
- Reconstruct nearby views around a fresh origin, reconnect IDs after creating
  entities, and restore steering state without resetting progression or islands.
- Use a versioned plain-data snapshot. Binary FileAccess.store_var() with object
  serialization disabled is the proposed format under user://. Keep a validated
  previous save and replace the main file only after a temporary write completes.
  Full save implementation and schema evolution belong to their own milestone.

10. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER AND ACCEPTANCE CHECKS

[ ] Build the ship/island scenes and a small authored starting fleet; establish
    the origin conversion functions and explicit owner/reference contracts.
[ ] Implement anchor feedback, moving destinations, heavy movement, and local
    avoidance. Then add orbit/selection/free-pan controls and the viewing sphere.
[ ] Wire coordinated origin shifts and logical island spawning. Compare average
    and anchor camera targets before declaring the camera choice settled.
[ ] Inspect git status before/after implementation and preserve unrelated files.
    Use a Godot 4.7-compatible console executable, serial launches, an absolute
    project path, task-scoped logs outside the repository, and process-local
    APPDATA isolation as required by AGENTS.md. Do not disturb the user's editor.
[ ] Run the headless editor import/check and inspect logs plus exit status for
    parser errors, missing resources, invalid UIDs, and startup failures.
[ ] Run the composed scene with rendering and inspect mouse and controller input,
    window resizing, snapping/following, right-button release, UI input capture,
    stick deadzones, both fleet-focus choices, and camera-sphere edges.
[ ] Exercise single/multiple ships, membership changes, removal of a followed
    ship, and an empty fleet. Verify that the anchor never jumps, reverses, or
    gains progression from camera movement or membership changes.
[ ] Hold ships behind the anchor and release them: the anchor slows and recovers
    smoothly, and ships catch up. Verify that destinations move with the anchor,
    arrival produces a nearby goal, and no immediate front-to-back trips occur.
[ ] Exercise head-on, crossing, overtaking, and close parallel passes; check for
    overlaps, symmetric deadlocks, jitter, unstable retargeting, and excessive
    clearance. Inspect heading at low speed, bounded pitch/bank, and no loops.
[ ] Add focused regression checks for signed coordinate normalization, invariant
    logical positions across rebasing, unchanged relative transforms/velocities,
    and no duplicate island spawning at boundaries. Use a task-sized check,
    not a general test framework.
[ ] Force several origin shifts and initialize at a very large signed segment
    index. Inspect rendering, picking, physics, camera bounds, steering, and spawn
    spacing before/after shifts. Verify long travel keeps live scenery bounded.
[ ] Review serialized scene/resource/project diffs, preserve generated .uid and
    source-adjacent import settings, keep temporary logs/captures out of commits,
    and report actual checks plus remaining limitations. Do not push without
    separate authorization.
